SCENE DESCRIPTION
A complete, readable commercial passenger-airplane cabin scene during an in-flight meal service, with BUSINESS CLASS in the front and ECONOMY CLASS farther back in the SAME aircraft. Use a gently elevated three-quarter cutaway/dollhouse view that opens the near wall and some roof to reveal the continuous interior, while retaining enough curved fuselage, real oval windows and overhead bins to make the airplane architecture unmistakable. This is one coherent cabin, not two unrelated images.

TARGET VOCABULARY / REQUIRED CONCEPTS
Show cabin, flight attendant, passenger, overhead bin, luggage, cart, meal, tray, seat, screen, headphones, magazine, window, cloud, wing, engine, business class, economy class, cup, water, juice, plate, open, serve, eat, drink, comfortable, wide, narrow. Supporting props may include a suitcase, seat belt, fork, pillow and blanket. All words are concepts to draw, never rendered text.

OBJECT VISIBILITY RULES
1. An adult traveler is standing in the aisle and OPENING ONE hinged OVERHEAD BIN, hand on its raised lid. Inside is a clearly visible rolling carry-on SUITCASE securely resting on the bin shelf. No falling bag.
2. A female FLIGHT ATTENDANT in a navy-and-gold professional uniform is SERVING a prepared MEAL TRAY from a wheeled airline service CART to an ECONOMY passenger. The cart is plainly visible with separate meal trays, a transparent clear-water pitcher and a separate transparent pitcher of orange juice. Keep the two pitchers distinguishable and visible.
3. A DIFFERENT flight attendant pours a drink from a small clear beverage pitcher into a CUP on a traveler's tray table. Natural hand placement, clear stream, pitcher and cup separated, not fused.
4. A seated business-class traveler is already EATING a meal with a FORK from a PLATE on a deployed tray table. The food must be recognizable and include a small bread roll and vegetables; no labels. A pillow and folded blanket rest on a separate business seat or its side shelf so the spacious seat shape remains readable.
5. ECONOMY seatbacks have visible rectangular entertainment SCREENS with simple scenery or abstract color images, no text. One economy passenger wears clearly visible over-ear HEADPHONES and DRINKS from a small cup held at the mouth; this is a different passenger from the meal recipient. Another nearby seatback has a MAGAZINE partially tucked in its lower literature pocket, with a plain illustrated cover and visible pages. Do not cover the screen with the magazine. Keep at least one complete seatback clear to identify all these features.
6. The BUSINESS seats in the front-left have broad padded seat cushions, wide armrests and generous legroom, arranged as two spacious premium seats. The ECONOMY area behind/right has narrower upright seats in closely spaced orderly rows. A subtle open curtain/partition marks the transition while the aisle continues between both areas. Differences must be visual, NO class signs.
7. Make at least one seat belt clearly visible across an unused seat cushion or resting neatly on an armrest, without hiding it under a person.
8. Outside the oval cabin WINDOWS, show bright white CLOUDS. In the near-side exterior revealed by the cutaway, immediately beyond the retained oval-window wall section, a recognizable WING extends outward and an ENGINE NACELLE hangs UNDER that wing. The angle must make the window-to-wing relationship visually clear. These are OUTSIDE the fuselage, never sitting inside the cabin. Show enough of both wing and engine to recognize each, not a distant sliver. A window view can show part of them; the cutaway also reveals their exterior position. Keep sky and clouds around these external structures.
